# ClearHead — Locked Decisions (v2)
*Updated May 21, 2026. The source of truth. Supersedes v1. Positioning lane is locked (behavior-first, anti-journaling, brain-dump). The exact subtitle is an A/B test — two lanes below, both strong, decided by launch data not gut.*

---

## The anchor
**Category:** a brain dump app.
**Name:** ClearHead
**One-liner:** Journaling for people who don't journal. Talk or type what's stuck in your head, and the app turns the mess into something clean you can actually use.
**Core promise:** Get it out. Move on.

### Subtitle — A/B test (pick the winner at launch, don't pre-decide)

**Lane A — Tidiness:** *Brain dumps, cleaned up.*
- Sells the **feature** — the AI does something to the mess, separates you from a plain voice memo. Dry, light, unbothered. Risk: leans productivity-shelf; sells the organizing, not the relief.

**Lane B — Relief:** *Say it. Feel lighter.* (alt: *Get it off your chest.*)
- Sells the **value** — why the buyer opens it at all. Risk: skews emotional/heavy, which this buyer avoids; can read like every other vent app.

*Both ride the same locked lane (behavior, not identity — never "you're the strong one"). The split is feature-forward vs. value-forward. **Test 2 doors, not 4** — a lead (Lane A) and one challenger (Lane B). On a solo budget, four messages = no clean signal and a muddy brand. A/B exists to find the winner, then concentrate — converge after the test, don't run every angle forever. Until then, default to Lane A in build copy because it's lighter and matches the tone rule — working anchor, not a blood oath.*

---

## Who it's for — a trait, not a demographic
The self-reliant one who bottles it. Cares about the people around them, which is why they won't unload on them. Tried the big wellness apps, found them bloated, bounced off. Not a man-thing or an age-thing — a wiring thing.

**The download trigger:** disillusionment, not crisis. "I tried the others, they were too much, none of them just let me get it out."

*This insight guides the product and the deeper "why." It does NOT go in the headline. The copy stays simple and lets the user fill in the rest.*

---

## The angle — front door vs. buried why
- **Front door (lead here):** behavior. "Stop typing angry essays into your Notes app." / "The easiest way to journal is to stop trying to journal."
- **Buried why (never the headline):** the social cost — get it out without it costing you anyone, without making it a whole conversation.
- Never pathologize ("are you anxious?"). Never flatter the identity ("you're the strong one") — it reads as a guess they'll roll their eyes at. *(This corrects v1, which wrongly led with the identity hook.)*

---

## The hard rule — non-negotiable
ClearHead never re-opens the lid. No notifications that resurface the user's pain. History is pull-only — there if they go looking, never pushed. The release stays a release.

---

## The hero loop
Open → talk or type the mess → AI cleans it up → save it or let it go. One sitting, no audience.

---

## The "oh damn" output structure (the feature that makes it more than a prettier Notes app)
After a dump, don't just return a transcript. Return:
- **What you said** — the cleaned-up version of the ramble.
- **What's underneath** — the core thing they're circling.
- **One thing to let go of** — a short, grounding line. *(Grounding, not advice. Never "you should." Never clinical.)*

Then two choices: **Save it** or **Let it go** (ephemeral mode).

---

## Differentiators worth building
- **Distillation** — the 10-minute ramble → the 3-part output above. The "oh damn" moment.
- **Ephemeral mode** — say it, watch it dissolve, nothing saved. Deliberate forgetting as a feature.
- **Pull-only look-back** — meaning, not a mood graph. Only when the user goes looking.
- **2–3 reflection modes** — stark and blunt in tone, never coach-y or clinical.

---

## Tone — non-negotiable
ClearHead reads the user's tone and meets it, then lifts. It acknowledges weight without absorbing it, and never goes heavier than what the user brought in. Steady, not somber. Warm, not tender. Dry over solemn. The goal is always lighter on the way out.

*Why: the heaviness is the exact thing the buyer avoids by not journaling. If the app feels somber — solemn prompts, tender therapist voice, candle-lit UI — you've recreated the thing they dodge. But cold or flip reads as not listening. Land between: acknowledge briefly, like a steady friend, then move toward lift. Calibrate to the dump — light in, light touch; heavy in, a real beat of acknowledgment first, then still aim up. The app is always lighter than the problem the user walked in with.*

**Applies to ad creative too — even the relief lane.** No 2am-crying-in-the-dark ads, no whispered "nothing I do is enough." Beautiful and somber is still somber, and it sells the wrong feeling. The app exists to make people feel *good* — get it out, feel lighter, get on with your day. Market the lift, never the spiral.

---

## Scope — V1
**IN — hero:** the core loop (voice + text dump, AI clean-up, the 3-part output), save/let-go, simple onboarding, paywall.
**IN — secondary (the keep-the-app reasons):** session history + gentle pull-only pattern-surfacing ("you've brought up your boss a few times lately"), sleep / white-noise sounds, a breathing tool. These are the features that make someone keep the app installed after they cancel — the insurance hook — and they're table-stakes users expect. Opt-in, never pushed, never crowding the hero. The data's already stored for the loop to work; this is about how it's surfaced.
**CUT:** streaks, sentiment scores, visible auto-tags, pattern insights *as the home-screen point*, gamification, pets, celebrity content, clinical language, social features, the "use it for anything" junk-drawer.

*Test for every feature: does it serve the dump-and-feel-lighter loop, and does it respect the close-the-book? Hero or quiet secondary — never center stage unless it's the loop.*
